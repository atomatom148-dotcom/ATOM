## 0. Why — measured, on the one clean session

All figures are read-only reads of the isolated simulator project `mhexfuamuwhzmszvojre`, the Render worker `srv-dabgssvavr4c73852m3g` logs, and `main`. No protected statistic was computed.

SIM-5 on 2026-09-04, regular hours (`resolution_target_at` in 13:30–20:00 UTC): 630 terminal resolutions, **190 `RESOLVED` (30.2%)**, 440 `UNRESOLVED_OBSERVATION_GAP`, **0 `UNRESOLVED_WINDOW_EXPIRED`**. By UTC hour: 30.3 / 32.4 / 30.9 / 32.3 / 23.8%. Across all sessions since activation: 195 resolved, 548 unresolved, every unresolved row a gap.

Resolved exit lag (`exit_quote_accepted_at − resolution_target_at`): min 0.035 s, p25 0.30, p50 0.71, p75 1.27, max 1.97 s. Provider-event lag is the same to within 40 ms. Transit (`accepted_at − provider_event`) on every selected quote is 33–46 ms, p50 35 ms — admission is prompt and there is no clock-skew clipping.

Worker telemetry (`SIM4_FAIL_CLOSED` lines): `quote_queue_full` 1,874 at 13:30:07Z, 16,669 at 14:00:00Z, 20,775 at 14:11:56Z on instance `w6k6p`; after the 15:40Z redeploy to `3fcc672`, instance `mqhmh` shows 39 at 15:44:58Z, 1,482 at 19:55:01Z, 7,972 at 20:00:29Z. No `quote_queue_full` line at all during 17:00–17:01Z. `quote_invalid` reached 7 on `w6k6p` and 60 on `mqhmh`. No `quote_lock_contention` or `socket_failures` lines. `quote_admitted` is counted (`quant/v9_sim4_worker.py:549`, `:1294`) but never logged.

Three facts are established by the merged code, independent of any hypothesis:

1. **No deadline drain for resolutions.** `_ready_loop` terminalizes a pending resolution the moment the derived clock passes `deadline_epoch_ns` (`quant/v9_sim4_worker.py:2433–2438` → `_terminalize_due_resolutions`, `:2159–2196`). Quotes reach a pending resolution only when the owner thread drains them from the bounded FIFO (`_retain_admitted_quote` → `_offer_quote_to_pending_resolutions`, `:2061–2090`). Any quote with `accepted_at` inside the closed window that is still in the FIFO at that instant is never offered. The SIM-4 entry path does the opposite: strict-greater sample under the admission mutex, watermark, drain through the watermark, then decide (`_deadline_sample` `:1302–1310`, `_drain_through` `:2048–2059`; SIM-3B §6). SIM-4A §3 and §5 never said which model the resolver follows; the implementation chose the one without the drain.
2. **The continuity proof cannot succeed on the first connection.** `_sip_observed_continuously` returns `False` whenever the SIP streak began before the runtime anchor (`:1499–1523`, guard `streak_start_ns < anchor.monotonic_ns`). The receiver sets readiness and opens the streak back-to-back on its own thread (`:931–932`); the owner thread captures the anchor only after readiness (`:1443–1462`, `:1526–1537`). The streak therefore precedes the anchor in the normal startup order, and every deadline without a quote on that connection becomes `UNRESOLVED_OBSERVATION_GAP`. `tests/test_v9_sim5_resolution.py` (the `pre_anchor_entry` case) asserts exactly this outcome, so it is a design decision, not a slip. This is why 548 of 548 unresolved rows are gaps and `UNRESOLVED_WINDOW_EXPIRED` has never been written. SIM-4A §5 intends the opposite: a connection that has stayed open since before the window *is* the proof.
3. **The admitted-quote rate is unobservable.** Only the fail-closed counters are logged. Whether the resolver is starved by FIFO backlog at the deadline (fact 1), by `put_nowait` overflow (`SIM4_EVENT_QUEUE_CAPACITY = 256`, drop-incoming — frozen by SIM-3A §11.4), or by sparse delivery on the socket cannot be separated from persisted data. The lag shape above is consistent with roughly one admitted quote per 2–3 s reaching the resolver; the flat hourly rate and the drop-free minute at 17:00Z argue against overflow being the whole story.

What this amendment does **not** do, and why: it does not change the two-second window (`RESOLUTION_WINDOW_SECONDS = 2`, `quant/v9_sim5_resolution.py:53`; enforced again by migration 031 `atom_v9_sim_resolutions_window_check` and by `quant/v9_sim4_entry.py:805`). Widening it is a schema change with its own migration and record-contract consequences, and the measurement in §3 decides whether it is even the binding constraint. It does not change the event-queue capacity or overflow rule (SIM-3A law). It does not touch statuses, record fields, hashes, canonicalization, migration 031, roles, credentials, services, or sources.

## 1. Change 1 — Resolution deadline sampling and drain (adds §3.1 to SIM-4A)

The SIM-5 resolver adopts the SIM-3B §6 deadline model verbatim for every pending resolution:

1. The owner thread observes the resolution deadline by acquiring the admission mutex, reading `monotonic_now_ns` only after acquisition, comparing the derived instant with `resolution_deadline_at`, and, when strictly greater, snapshotting the last admitted sequence as the watermark before releasing the mutex. Equality is pending and schedules the existing bounded recheck.
2. It then drains every admitted envelope through that watermark, offering each drained quote to every pending resolution exactly as `_offer_quote_to_pending_resolutions` does today. A drained envelope with a sequence above the watermark is the existing generation failure.
3. Only after the drain completes does it classify: `RESOLVED` with the deterministic minimum `(accepted_at, provider_event_ns, quote_id)` among valid in-window quotes; otherwise §5's unresolved classification.
4. A quote whose `accepted_at` lies inside the closed window is a candidate whether it was drained before or after the wall-clock deadline. A quote admitted after the watermark cannot have `accepted_at` inside the window (admission stamps `accepted_at` under the same mutex, SIM-3B §6) and is excluded by construction.
5. Failure to complete the drain produces no terminal decision. The frozen restart rules then apply, and §5 already resolves unknown coverage as a gap.

When a SIM-4 entry deadline and a SIM-5 resolution deadline fall at the same derived instant, one sample/watermark/drain serves both; classification of resolutions precedes entry selection. The order is deterministic and must be tested.

Window bounds, exit side, causal floor, ordering tuple, return mathematics, hash, `resolution_id`, and the record's field order are unchanged. A `RESOLVED` record produced under this section is byte-identical in form to one produced today.

## 2. Change 2 — Continuous observation across the runtime anchor (amends §5)

Replace the second bullet's "or another provable observation discontinuity" reading with this exact rule:

Continuous authorized SIP observation over `[resolution_target_at, resolution_deadline_at]` is proven when one authenticated, subscribed WebSocket connection remained open — no disconnect, reconnect, stop, ownership loss, generation failure, or worker restart — from an instant at or before `resolution_target_at` through an instant at or after `resolution_deadline_at`.

A connection opened before the runtime anchor was captured counts as observed from the anchor instant onward. The anchor is the earliest instant the worker can express in UTC; observation before it is not claimed and not needed, because no window can be registered before the anchor exists. The exact pre-anchor path is frozen by the connection-transition rule below: the open callback publishes its monotonic start and generation as pre-anchor state, and anchor capture atomically converts that still-open generation to a derived streak beginning exactly at the anchor. The guard that fails a pre-anchor streak closed is removed, and the test asserting `UNRESOLVED_OBSERVATION_GAP` for that case is replaced by tests for the outcomes in §6.

An open connection is necessary, not sufficient. Observation also requires that the worker admitted, and kept, every quote the connection delivered inside the interval. Each of the following is an observation discontinuity at its instant: an admission refused because the event queue was full or the admission mutex was contended (the SIM-3A §11.4 drop paths counted as `quote_queue_full` and `quote_lock_contention`), and an admitted envelope dropped from the quote buffer at capacity (`quote_buffer_full`).

Loss instants. For a queue-full refusal the instant is the refused quote's `accepted_at`, already derived under the admission mutex. For a potential mutex-contention refusal, the receiver first registers the admission attempt under the loss-record fence defined below and samples `attempt_started_ns` there; if the immediately following non-blocking admission acquire fails, that pre-attempt sample is the refusal instant. It is never replaced by a later post-failure clock read. The receiver derives the epoch through the runtime anchor at nanosecond precision without rounding; with no anchor, admission is disabled, no window can exist, and nothing is recorded. For a buffer-capacity drop the instant is the dropped envelope's `accepted_at`. Every instant is kept in a bounded, secret-free record of exactly 65,536 instants (`SIM4_ADMISSION_LOSS_CAPACITY`, frozen like the other SIM-4 bounded structures so every implementation enters the overflow rule at the same point; at the 2026-09-04 refusal rate — 18,901 refusals on instance `w6k6p` between 13:30:07Z and 14:11:56Z, about 7.5 per second — the record holds more than two hours of refusals between applications) and is applied — at every registration and before every classification — to every window pending at that moment: a window is marked when `resolution_target_at <= instant <= resolution_deadline_at` for a refusal instant. An instant may be discarded only after that application, and only once it is older than the retention bound below plus one second, so no window can lose the evidence before it is classified. A marked window with no admitted candidate is `UNRESOLVED_OBSERVATION_GAP`, never `UNRESOLVED_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, because the worker cannot prove the refused quote was not a valid exit. A marked window that holds a valid admitted candidate still resolves: §3's candidate set is, and always was, the admitted quotes, and a quote that was never admitted is not a candidate. That clause applies only to a resolution whose coverage is proven. When the registration rule below finds a resolution's coverage unproven — registration after `resolution_target_at` with a loss instant of any kind in `[resolution_target_at, registration]`, or registration more than 2 seconds after `resolution_target_at`, or registration with no runtime anchor — the registration rule controls: the resolution is `UNRESOLVED_OBSERVATION_GAP` regardless of any candidate, whether replayed from the buffer at registration or drained later. Classification therefore tests unproven coverage first, then a candidate, then the continuity proof, so the two rules can never yield conflicting outcomes for one resolution. A buffer-capacity drop is not applied to any resolution already pending when that envelope was offered: the resolver evaluated that envelope before retention dropped it, so the drop creates no unknown observation for that pending resolution even when the envelope was rejected as a candidate. Its instant is retained solely for registrations that occur after the drop, where it participates in the elapsed-coverage test below because that newly registered resolution never received the evicted envelope. The loss record therefore tags each instant by kind and, for `quote_buffer_full`, by the registration-generation cutoff at the drop; record application marks only registrations later than that cutoff, never a resolution pending at the cutoff.

Admission-attempt publication fence and loss-record linearization. Quote submission remains the exact nonblocking SIM-3A §11.4 path: the sole SIP receiver never acquires, waits on, or notifies a mutex/condition other than attempting the existing admission mutex nonblockingly, and its added work is bounded in-process construction plus fixed-slot publication. A fixed 65,536-slot single-writer admission-attempt journal (`SIM4_ADMISSION_ATTEMPT_CAPACITY = 65_536`) carries monotonically increasing generations. Immediately before each nonblocking admission acquire, the receiver allocates the next generation, initializes its indexed slot, and publishes that generation in state `IN_FLIGHT_UNSAMPLED` before reading the clock. It then reads `attempt_started_ns`, writes the sample, and publishes state `IN_FLIGHT_SAMPLED`; with no intervening application operation it performs the existing nonblocking acquire. If that clock read raises before a sample exists, a `finally` path publishes terminal `UNSAMPLED_ERROR` for the visible generation and propagates the original error. `UNSAMPLED_ERROR` is an unplaceable observation discontinuity, not an indefinitely in-flight attempt; the owner converts it to the fail-closed interval defined below using its post-drain upper-bound sample. If the receiver is descheduled before generation publication, any later sample is necessarily later than an owner's already-taken application sample. If it is descheduled after generation publication, the owner's fence includes it and must await both the sample and terminal outcome. Thus no attempt can retain a pre-application timestamp while remaining outside the fence. It otherwise publishes, in that same slot, exactly one of four sampled terminal outcomes: `LOCK_CONTENTION` with the unchanged pre-attempt sample; `QUEUE_FULL` with the `accepted_at` stamped by the existing admission rule; `ADMISSION_REJECTED` for every other non-admitted return or exception after the mutex was acquired, using `accepted_at` when it was successfully stamped and otherwise the unchanged pre-attempt sample; or `ADMITTED` only after successful enqueue, with no loss instant. Every outcome except `ADMITTED` is an observation discontinuity. A `finally`-equivalent publication is mandatory: no return, unsampled or sampled clock failure, raised build/enqueue exception, admission-disabled race, ownership/generation failure or shutdown path may leave a published generation in either in-flight state; the original failure still propagates after terminal publication. Terminal fields are written before the terminal state is published. These fixed-index assignments are the only added receiver operations; they do no I/O, allocation proportional to traffic, logging, retry, sleep or blocking synchronization. CPython 3.12 under the GIL is the frozen publication implementation: the receiver is the sole writer, the owner is the sole reader, slot field assignments complete while the GIL is held, and the terminal state is the publication field. Any additional producer, absent/corrupt generation, overwritten expected generation or out-of-order state fails closed as journal overflow over the same relevance interval defined below; it is never skipped. A buffer-capacity drop arises on the owner path and is appended to the bounded loss record while the owner holds the continuity mutex below.

Every registration and classification first takes its own registration or strict-greater deadline sample under the admission mutex and releases that mutex. It then snapshots the journal's highest published generation as its fence. On the owner path only, without holding the admission or continuity mutex, it consumes every generation through that fence in order into an owner-local fixed batch of exactly 65,536 entries (`SIM4_ADMISSION_BATCH_CAPACITY = 65_536`) and yields the GIL while awaiting an `IN_FLIGHT_UNSAMPLED` or `IN_FLIGHT_SAMPLED` slot's required next publication; the receiver therefore never waits behind classification. The owner may not persist a terminal decision until every generation through the fence is terminal. There is no timeout or skipped generation. A slot mismatch/overwrite invokes the fail-closed overflow interval rather than guessing an outcome. After the drain, the owner acquires the continuity mutex exactly once, transfers the complete local loss batch into the guarded bounded loss record, applies that record, and reads connection state atomically before release. The local batch is never shared or treated as the guarded record. Its capacity exactly equals the journal capacity; exceeding it invokes the same fail-closed interval at the same deterministic point in every implementation. Thus every guarded-record mutation occurs while holding its guarding mutex without adding any receiver-side mutex acquisition. An attempt published before the fence can be descheduled before its acquire or terminal publication but cannot escape that application. An attempt published after the snapshot necessarily has an `attempt_started_ns` later than the application's admission-mutex sample and cannot be an in-window loss for the deadline or elapsed-registration interval that sample decides. Classification cannot persist until the journal drain, loss application and connection-state read are complete. No telemetry path weakens this fence.

Connection-transition and anchor linearization. One continuity mutex, distinct from the admission mutex and never acquired by the SIP quote-submission path, guards the bounded loss record, runtime-anchor publication, and the complete authenticated/subscribed connection generation/open-streak state. The authenticated/subscribed socket-open publication is ordered before the readiness signal that can wake the owner: the receiver must complete the open callback's guarded state publication and release the continuity mutex before `_ready.set()` (or any equivalent readiness notification). The first application operation of every socket-open, disconnect, reconnect or stop callback is to acquire this mutex and read `monotonic_ns()` while held. If the runtime anchor exists, the callback derives the transition instant through it and publishes the whole transition before release. If the initial socket-open precedes the anchor, the callback instead advances the generation and publishes exactly `PRE_ANCHOR_OPEN` plus that monotonic start; it performs no impossible UTC derivation and does not fail the streak. No disconnect/reconnect/stop before anchor may be collapsed into that state: each publishes its generation/state monotonically under the same mutex.

After readiness wakes it, runtime-anchor capture requires that the corresponding authenticated/subscribed open generation is already published; missing, closed or mismatched readiness/open state fails closed and cannot enable admission. Runtime-anchor capture then acquires the continuity mutex before it enables admission or permits a window registration. It atomically publishes the anchor's monotonic/UTC pair and converts connection state. If and only if the current generation is still the same authenticated/subscribed `PRE_ANCHOR_OPEN` generation and its monotonic start is not later than the anchor monotonic sample, it becomes `OPEN` with derived streak start exactly equal to the anchor UTC instant. A later-starting open is derived normally; a closed, changed, invalid or future-start state remains unable to prove coverage. Anchor publication and conversion complete before release, and admission enablement follows release. Thus no registration or quote admission observes a half-converted state. The anchor is written once; a second/different anchor is fail-closed.

Transition failure is fail-closed. The guarded callback routine preinitializes all state needed for a total publication. If its monotonic clock read or ordinary transition construction fails after acquiring the continuity mutex, a `finally`-equivalent path advances the generation, closes the open streak, and publishes terminal `TRANSITION_ERROR` before release; it carries no invented instant and creates an unplaceable lossy interval from the last clean transition (or the runtime anchor if none) through the owner's next post-drain sample. Registration/classification treats an overlapping `TRANSITION_ERROR` as unproven continuity. If the mutex cannot be acquired or even this minimal terminal publication cannot complete, `_notify_observation()` must not swallow the `BaseException` and continue: it invokes the existing fatal-stop/ownership-loss path, prevents further admission and terminal classification, and the worker stop is an observation gap. Only after one of those fail-closed outcomes may the receiver lifecycle continue or terminate. For later successful transitions, the under-mutex clock read is the detection instant; no earlier callback-entry, kernel or provider time is claimed. After the admission-attempt fence has drained, registration and classification inspect and freeze loss/connection state under this same continuity mutex. A transition sampled before that read is published and visible; one sampled after it is later than the application's admission-mutex sample and cannot be inserted into the closed interval already decided. Thus a detected in-window disconnect/reconnect cannot race behind an immutable `UNRESOLVED_WINDOW_EXPIRED` decision.

Overflow and unplaceable loss. If the journal, local batch or bounded record overflows, the worker can no longer preserve every precise instant. After draining the complete fence as far as its fixed storage permits, the owner samples `drain_completed_ns` and derives its UTC instant through the anchor while holding no continuity mutex. The lossy interval begins one relevance period (the retention bound plus one second) before the previous application. Its inclusive upper bound is the maximum of: the application sample; every known sampled loss instant in the discarded/overflowed fence, including one sampled after the application instant; and the derived drain-completion instant whenever an `UNSAMPLED_ERROR` or an overwritten/unreadable sampled field prevents exact placement. The owner then acquires the continuity mutex, retains this complete interval, and applies the same inclusive overlap test: every pending window whose closed interval overlaps it is marked, and every registration whose `[resolution_target_at, registration]` overlaps it has unproven coverage. A window that lies entirely after the complete lossy interval is untouched; a loss before a window begins interrupts no part of it. The interval is retained through later applications until its upper bound is older than the relevance period, so a post-application loss swallowed by overflow remains available to mark a later registration/window.

First overflow. Admission is enabled at SIP readiness, before activation capture, and activation capture may keep yielding while the receiver admits and refuses quotes (SIM-3B §6–§7); recovered resolutions are registered only after occupancy loads. The record can therefore overflow before any application has occurred. When the application handling overflow or unplaceable evidence has no previous application, the lower bound is the runtime anchor instant — the instant admission was enabled — because no loss can precede it. Its upper bound is the complete computed upper bound from the preceding paragraph: the maximum of the detecting application sample, every known discarded sampled loss, and the post-drain sample required by `UNSAMPLED_ERROR`, `TRANSITION_ERROR`, or an unreadable instant. The first interval is therefore `[anchor, computed upper bound]`; every recovered or later registration/window whose relevant closed interval overlaps it has unproven coverage, and only a window whose target lies after that computed upper bound is untouched. The overflow state is retained, never dropped: the overflow flag survives until the application that consumes it, and the resulting lossy interval survives every later application until its upper end is older than the relevance period, by which time every pending window overlapping it has been marked and every later registration overlapping it is already unproven by the two-second registration bound.

Registration relative to the window. A pending resolution is normally registered at the durable `ENTERED` commit, before its `resolution_target_at`. SIM-1 bounds `eligible_at − cutoff_at` from below only, so registration may fall after `resolution_target_at`; the elapsed part of such a window was not being watched. The registration instant is sampled under the admission mutex, after the pending record exists, through the same monotonic-to-derived mapping as `accepted_at` at nanosecond precision without rounding. At every registration the worker offers each retained quote-buffer envelope to the new pending resolution. Coverage of the elapsed part is proven only when all three conditions hold: (1) the registration instant is within the quote-buffer retention bound of the target — registration minus `resolution_target_at` not greater than 2 seconds, inclusive, so no in-window envelope could yet have been evicted; (2) no loss instant of any kind satisfies `resolution_target_at <= instant <= registration`; and (3) under the connection-transition linearization above, the current single authenticated/subscribed connection generation's published open streak began at or before `resolution_target_at` and remained the same open generation through the registration sample, with no startup, disconnect, reconnect, stop, ownership loss, generation failure or worker restart in that elapsed interval. Otherwise the resolution's coverage is unproven and it terminalizes as `UNRESOLVED_OBSERVATION_GAP` at its deadline regardless of any quote offered at registration or afterwards, because an earlier valid exit may have been discarded and §3's first-quote rule could not be honored. A registration whose target precedes the runtime anchor, whose worker started after the target, whose current connection opened after the target, or which has no runtime anchor cannot prove condition (3) and has unproven coverage. Unproven coverage is decided at registration and is never revised by later quotes; it takes precedence over the marked-window clause above.

Everything else in §5 stands: startup after the interval began, restart, ownership loss, and any disconnect/reconnect inside the interval are gaps; unknown coverage is a gap; pre-activation windows cannot be reconstructed; both statuses are terminal evidence.

## 3. Change 3 — Ingress observability (adds §9.1 to SIM-4A)

The worker logs the existing `quote_admitted` counter with the same fixed-field, secret-free discipline as `SIM4_FAIL_CLOSED`: one line at every telemetry tick of the existing main loop (`SIM4_TELEMETRY_LOG_INTERVAL_SECONDS`, 1.000 s, polled at 0.25 s, so consecutive ticks are nominally 1.000–1.250 s apart) from the first tick at which admission is enabled — the runtime anchor captured, SIM-3B §6, which precedes activation capture while the status enum still reads `RECOVERING` — until the worker stops, whether or not any value changed and whatever the status enum shows, of the form `SIM4_INGRESS status=<status> quote_admitted=<int> delta=<int> quote_queue_full=<int> quote_lock_contention=<int>`, where `delta` is the change in `quote_admitted` since the previous line and may be `0`, and the two loss counters are the existing cumulative fail-closed counters repeated unconditionally. They are repeated because the change-only `SIM4_FAIL_CLOSED` path advances its snapshot even when the log call fails, so a loss increment that coincides with a transient logging failure is never emitted there; in the heartbeat every later line carries the cumulative value, so nothing is lost. Before admission is enabled no quote can be admitted and no line is emitted. Because it is unconditional while admission is enabled, the line is also the ingress heartbeat, and the heartbeat contract is measurable by the log's own timestamps: two consecutive `SIM4_INGRESS` lines from one instance more than 2.000 s apart are a telemetry gap — the bound is the worst nominal spacing (1.250 s) plus headroom, and any larger spacing means a full interval passed with no line. A minute of zero admissions is therefore distinguishable from a minute the worker did not report. (On the 2026-09-04 session the change-only `SIM4_FAIL_CLOSED` lines of instance `w6k6p` carry Render timestamps 1.0005 s apart tick after tick, e.g. 13:31:18.110 → 13:31:19.110 → 13:31:20.111, so the log timestamps track the tick cadence to milliseconds.) No new counter, no payload, no timestamp beyond the log's own, no configuration, URL, or exception text. SIM-3B §9's "every fail-closed event is observable through secret-free counters and logs" is extended to the success path so the admitted-quote rate per minute of regular hours becomes a number, and the loss counters become available per minute from the same unconditional stream.

Purpose: with `quote_admitted`, `quote_queue_full`, and `quote_lock_contention` on one unconditional line, one session separates the three explanations in §0 fact 3 without touching the ledger.

## 6. Required tests

- Drain: a quote admitted with `accepted_at` inside the window but still in the FIFO when the derived clock passes the deadline is selected; the resolution is `RESOLVED` with that quote, not unresolved.
- Watermark: a quote admitted after the watermark is not a candidate; a drained sequence above the watermark is the existing generation failure; deadline equality schedules the bounded recheck and produces no terminal decision.
- Determinism: two in-window quotes drained in either order select the same `(accepted_at, provider_event_ns, quote_id)` minimum.
- Shared instant: a SIM-4 entry deadline and a SIM-5 resolution deadline at the same derived instant use one watermark; resolution classification precedes entry selection; both outcomes are unchanged from separate handling.
- Continuity: streak opened before the anchor, window entirely after the anchor, no quote → `UNRESOLVED_WINDOW_EXPIRED`; window beginning before the anchor → `UNRESOLVED_OBSERVATION_GAP`; disconnect inside the window → gap; startup after the window began → gap; a reconnect that opens a new streak after the window began → gap.
- Admission loss: a queue-full refusal inside a window that holds no admitted candidate → gap; the same refusal outside every window leaves a quote-less window `UNRESOLVED_WINDOW_EXPIRED`; a refusal inside a window that holds a valid admitted candidate → `RESOLVED` with that candidate; a mutex-contention refusal is treated identically to a queue-full refusal.
- Registration: registration at or before the target is unchanged; registration after the target but within 2 s of it, with one unchanged authenticated/subscribed connection generation open from at or before the target through registration, no loss, and a valid in-window quote still retained in the buffer → `RESOLVED` with the first such quote. Registration more than 2 s after the target, startup/runtime anchor after the target, current connection opened after the target, or disconnect/reconnect/stop/ownership/generation/restart between target and registration → gap even when a valid in-window candidate is retained and replayed. Registration within 2 s of the target with a buffer-capacity drop or admission refusal between target and registration likewise → gap; the registration rule controls over the marked-window clause.
- Telemetry: the `SIM4_INGRESS` line is emitted at every interval once admission is enabled — including while the status enum reads `RECOVERING` and including when `delta` is `0` — and never before admission is enabled; it carries only the fixed fields, including the cumulative `quote_queue_full` and `quote_lock_contention` values, and can never contain configuration, URLs, payloads, exceptions, credentials, or tokens.
- Loss record: every nonblocking admission attempt publishes a pre-attempt monotonic sample, monotonically increasing generation and terminal outcome in the fixed 65,536-slot single-writer journal without acquiring or waiting on a new mutex/condition. Registration/classification snapshots a generation fence and the owner drains every generation through it before record application; an in-flight attempt delays only the owner, never the receiver, and no skipped/overwritten generation or timeout is accepted. A loss instant is applied to every pending window before it can be discarded; journal or loss-record overflow marks only pending windows overlapping the lossy interval and leaves a window entirely after it untouched; a registration whose `[target, registration]` overlaps the lossy interval has unproven coverage; an overflow or unplaceable loss before any application — refusals or transition errors during activation capture, before the first recovered resolution is registered — produces the lossy interval `[anchor, computed upper bound]`, retained past that application, so a recovered/later window overlapping it has unproven coverage even with a retained in-window candidate, while a window whose target lies after that complete upper bound is untouched; the capacities are exactly `SIM4_ADMISSION_ATTEMPT_CAPACITY = 65_536` and `SIM4_ADMISSION_LOSS_CAPACITY = 65_536`.
- Forced interleaving and startup: publish an in-window attempt through `IN_FLIGHT_UNSAMPLED` and `IN_FLIGHT_SAMPLED`, fail its nonblocking acquire, then deschedule the receiver before terminal publication. The owner's classification freezes a fence containing that generation and produces no terminal decision while the receiver is held; once the receiver publishes `LOCK_CONTENTION`, the owner drains the unchanged pre-attempt instant and the window is `UNRESOLVED_OBSERVATION_GAP`, never `UNRESOLVED_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Exercise every acquired-but-not-admitted return and exception and require `ADMISSION_REJECTED` publication in all sampled cases before propagation, with no generation left in flight. Force the clock read itself to raise after `IN_FLIGHT_UNSAMPLED` publication, require terminal `UNSAMPLED_ERROR`, and prove the owner finishes its fence and creates the fail-closed interval through its post-drain upper-bound sample. Hold classification in loss application and prove a new receiver attempt still reaches and completes the nonblocking admission path within the preserved SIM-3A ingress latency contract; classification never owns a receiver-needed lock. Prove the owner drains into a local batch without the continuity mutex and mutates/applies the guarded record only after acquiring that mutex once. Deschedule once after `IN_FLIGHT_UNSAMPLED` publication but before the clock sample and once before generation publication; require the former generation to be fenced and awaited and the latter attempt's eventual sample to be later than the application sample. Force slot/local-batch wrap, overflow or mismatch at the exact shared 65,536-entry bound; include a discarded sampled loss later than the application sample and require the retained fail-closed interval's upper bound to equal that later instant, then prove a later overlapping registration is marked. Offer an invalid envelope to an already-pending resolution immediately before buffer eviction and prove the drop does not mark it; register a later overlapping resolution and prove the retained tagged drop makes its elapsed coverage unproven. Separately order disconnect detection on both sides of classification's frozen state read. Force the transition clock and ordinary guarded construction to fail and require `TRANSITION_ERROR`, a closed streak, advanced generation and a retained unplaceable interval; force mutex/minimal-publication failure and require fatal stop with no further admission or terminal classification, never a swallowed callback error. Finally, require authenticated/subscribed open publication and mutex release before readiness notification; deschedule the receiver at the former `_ready.set()`/callback boundary and prove the owner cannot capture an anchor first. Then capture the anchor concurrently with no registration/admission escape and require atomic conversion of that same still-open `PRE_ANCHOR_OPEN` generation to an `OPEN` streak beginning exactly at the anchor; missing publication, a pre-anchor disconnect/reconnect or changed generation must fail closed and must not be clamped into continuity.

Minute buckets and completeness. Regular hours are the 390 one-minute buckets from 09:30:00 through 15:59:59 America/New_York on the session day, assigned by each log line's own timestamp. The per-minute admitted count of a bucket is the cumulative `quote_admitted` at the last `SIM4_INGRESS` line in that bucket minus the cumulative at the last line in the preceding bucket; for the first bucket the baseline is the last `SIM4_INGRESS` line before 09:30:00 from the same instance, and at least one such pre-open line must exist. The per-minute `quote_queue_full` and `quote_lock_contention` counts are taken from the same lines by the same subtraction. A bucket in which a counter did not move contributes `0` for that counter. Complete telemetry means one worker instance for the whole session — no restart or deploy inside regular hours — the pre-open baseline line, and the §3 heartbeat contract held without exception from the baseline line through the first `SIM4_INGRESS` line at or after 16:00:00 America/New_York: no two consecutive `SIM4_INGRESS` lines from that instance more than 2.000 s apart by their own timestamps. That contract implies at least one line in every one of the 390 buckets and bounds what the last-line subtraction can shift across any bucket boundary to the admissions of at most the final 2.000 s of the earlier bucket, which is the frozen tolerance of the bucket calculation. A missing baseline, a bucket with no line, or any spacing greater than 2.000 s is a telemetry gap, not a zero, and the ingress-rate threshold is then not met.

Median convention. The p50 is the lower median of the 390 per-minute integer values: sort them ascending and take the 195th value (nearest rank, ⌈0.5 × 390⌉ = 195); the two middle values are not averaged and nothing is interpolated. The ingress-rate comparison is made on that integer against 300, before any conversion to quotes per second and before any display rounding. The receipt's min and p90 use the same nearest-rank convention: rank 1 and rank ⌈0.9 × 390⌉ = 351.

Resolution-coverage denominator. For each horizon, the denominator is every `ENTERED` entry whose `resolution_target_at` fell inside regular hours of the session, whether or not a terminal resolution row exists for it; the numerator is those with a `RESOLVED` terminal row. Any such entry with no terminal row at receipt time fails acceptance outright, because a stuck open entry occupies its horizon and suppresses every later entry, so a high share over the few that terminalized proves nothing.

Minimum denominator. A share over a handful of entries substantiates nothing about a session, so the coverage threshold is evaluated only when the denominator is at least 200 at 30S and at least 100 at 1M. The 2026-09-04 regular-hours denominators were 351 and 203; the minimums are the floor below which SIM-3 intent delivery, not resolution, was the session's limiting factor. A session with a smaller denominator at either gated horizon is diagnostic and does not close the repair, whatever its share.

Decision rule, stated now so it cannot drift (narrowed by ChatGPT Pro on adoption, 2026-09-08; the minimum denominator added on Codex review):

This repair closes only when, on one complete regular session, all of the following hold: (a) the resolution-coverage threshold — `RESOLVED` share, over the denominator above, of at least 90% — is met at 30S and at 1M, each individually, with no overdue open entry at any horizon; (b) the minimum denominator above is met at 30S and at 1M; and (c) the ingress-rate threshold — `quote_admitted` p50 of at least 5 quotes per second, i.e. the lower median defined above of at least 300 per minute over the 390 buckets — is met with complete telemetry as defined above.
